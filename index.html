Hello! I'm Shashiwadana Nirmani

PhD Researcher · Software Engineer

I am a PhD researcher in Software Engineering at Software Engineering Innovation Lab, School of Information Technology, Deakin University, Australia. My research interests are in Open Source Software (OSS) ecosystems, empirical software engineering, and human-centered software engineering. I investigate how human factors influence participation in OSS projects. By integrating behavioural and social factors into OSS project recommendation systems, I aim to develop more personalized, human-aware approaches that better support software practitioners than traditional recommendation techniques based solely on technical characteristics.

Prior to commencing my PhD, I worked as a Software Engineer, gaining over two years of industry experience developing software solutions in Agile environments.

Alongside my research, I work as a Graduate Researcher Teaching Fellow at Deakin University. I enjoy supporting student learning, sharing practical software engineering knowledge, and contributing to the academic community through both teaching and research.

Research

Research Projects

Funded Project

Understanding Age-Related Discussions in Mobile App Reviews

Investigates how age-related needs and concerns appear in mobile app reviews. By analysing 4,163 reviews and comparing machine learning, deep learning, and large language models, we developed an approach for detecting age-related discussions and identified key concerns around content, accessibility, age verification, privacy, safety, and social interactions.

PhD Study 1

Task Recommendation Systems for Crowdsourced Software Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review

A systematic literature review of 65 studies examining how software tasks and projects are recommended to contributors. The study identifies commonly used recommendation approaches, data sources, platforms, and limitations, while highlighting the need to better incorporate human factors into future recommendation systems.

Publication ↗Dataset ↗
PhD Study 2

Understanding What Motivates Contributors to Choose OSS Projects

Investigates how contributors' motivations and demographic backgrounds relate to the types of OSS projects they prefer. A survey of 208 software practitioners compares newcomers and experienced OSS contributors, revealing how motivations such as learning, career advancement, enjoyment, reputation, and financial incentives relate to different project characteristic preferences.

Publication ↗Dataset ↗
PhD Study 3

Understanding How Team Dynamics Influence OSS Project Selection

Investigates how the social and collaborative environment of OSS projects influences contributors' decisions to join. A survey of 198 OSS practitioners found that responsiveness, clear communication, and respectful interactions are particularly important, while preferences for team dynamics also vary depending on contributors' motivations.
